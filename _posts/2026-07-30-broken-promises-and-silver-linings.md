Haven't written anything lengthy and tedious lately. Admittedly, nothing after the shocking passing away of Steve Wright and my burdensome, depressing and lost uni years. But I have this sense of feeling that I simply couldn't hold myself anymore lately, I simply willing to be as much catharic as possible like my teenage year... partly because of findling the grand truth that human life is short and brutal, along with the discovery of the fragility of the cards of our life and how the most tedious and mundane daily routine is ironically, actually, the most precious.

Perhaps I should blame all of this on Elaine Page and her stupid Sunday playlist. Maybe Matt should also take the blame. And the emerging colour of silver from the mirror every morning. But all of a sudden, I think I discovered the tragedy of the ephemeral lifetime not because we could never reach the greatness that we used to envision. On the contrary, it's all those tiniest things that make us linger and feel unforgiven. One wise sentence, one tight hug, one unintentional touch of fingertips, one last look from the terminal gate... All those quirky moments are, ironically, the best moments of everyone's everyday lives.

Look, I just can't stop mumbling around those sad terms and depressing thoughts. But I simply can't walk away from all these, dear you. It's too heavy, and the road is too long.

And all of a sudden, the story of the *Unlikely Pilgrimage of Harold Fry* hit me, deep inside, to the core of all the sorrow. Sometimes we just can't balance life and aims and end up losing all. Maybe the whole purpose of life is to keep losing. I don't know, and I am so lost in the quest to write myself out. But by looking at other people's lives with a microscope, inspecting the gains and losses, the loved and hated, at least I have life to embrace everything circling a new meaning o around the radius of our life. From broken promises to silver linings, dear you, it's better no day but today. (And Matt, you better get well soon. I surely miss your wise dialogues and razor-sharp observations. You haven't given enough to this world, so get up and keep talking.)

In the meantime, recently, I brought up a paper-based book called "Under the Volcano: the story of Bali". But I am not going to talk about Bali. I would like to say things about Indonesia and the people overall. At the very moment, I am on the way out of Jakarta, from Taipei, but you know what, I think I may have lost a piece or two of my soul in the land that contains not only the morning of the world, but the sunset as well.

Someone said this to me: "It's the smile, the childish smile. Of course you are able to find this kind of smile among children all over the world, but you can only find this kind of smile among faces in adults, men and women, young and old, in Indonesia". And I am completely sold.

Dear you, I don't expect you to understand this contradiction. Life is hard here. People rarely sleep, and the pressure from life seems endless, but they have a guitar, food, and, I think most importantly, the spirit of nomads, which is that men are accustomed to the environment but not vice versa. That makes them embrace life to the fullest and smile. That's the smile we no longer remember, that's the eyesight we already lost, that's the mindset we buried long ago with our childish dream of pure love, that's the song of commoners' life.

And that's the moment I tossed a silver penny to the wishing well. Next, the wrinkle on the surface, the sound of touching bottom, and the emergence of the genie. I can't tell you the story of the genie, so I tell my thoughts instead. Don't blame me, blame the city, the sunset from the misty horizon, which reflects the dreams and sorrows of this country in fighting, but also the most mundane struggle of each citizen, from the bay side to mountaineers.

And I tried my best to breathe in the most of it. The breath of the genie. Raise both fingers and click, to remember the eyes of the genie. Maybe the wish won't come true, maybe the promises are deemed to be broken, maybe what's been silver is never the lining but pennies in the well, but who cares, anymore?

We've been through too much. At least we were there, had the chance to dance around destiny, from the sunset of the horizon to the morning of the world, dear you, even just one night, one sight.

Here's one for the road.
